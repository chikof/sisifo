01 — How it works
01
Publish your files
Drop a folder into Sísifo. Every file is hashed using BLAKE3 and stored as a content-addressed blob. A signed manifest ties them together.
02
Get a permanent address
Your site's manifest hash becomes its address. Permanent, unforgeable, and tied to your ed25519 identity. No DNS. No registrar.
03
Peers spread the word
Anyone who visits your site starts seeding it. The iroh DHT routes requests to whoever has the content — the more visitors, the more resilient.
02 — Features
Content addressing
Sites are addressed by what they contain, not where they live. The hash is the address — tamper-proof and permanent.
Persistent daemon
Run sisid as a systemd user service to seed your sites 24/7, even when the browser is closed.
Built in Rust
The node, gateway, publisher, and daemon are all written in Rust. Minimal memory footprint, no runtime, no GC pauses.
Signed manifests
Every site manifest is signed with your ed25519 keypair. Visitors can verify authorship without trusting a third party.
NAT traversal
iroh's QUIC hole-punching gets through most NATs. DERP relay fallback handles the rest — peers connect regardless of network topology.
Normal websites
Sites are plain HTML, CSS, and JS served via a local gateway. No new format to learn — if it runs in a browser, it runs in Sísifo.

Publish in seconds.

Select a folder, give your site a name, and hit publish. Sísifo hashes every file, builds a signed manifest, and announces it to the DHT.

Your site is live the moment the first peer fetches it.
